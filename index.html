Index / Data Series Description
U.S. Equity Indices
Dow Jones Industrial Average (DJIA)A price-weighted index of 30 large, blue-chip U.S. companies spanning major industries, widely used as a barometer of the U.S. stock market's overall health.
Dow Jones Transportation Average (DJTA)A price-weighted index of 20 major U.S. transportation companies — including airlines, railroads, and trucking firms — often watched as a leading indicator of economic activity.
Dow Jones Utilities AverageA price-weighted index of 15 large U.S. utility companies, commonly used to track the performance of the defensive utilities sector.
NASDAQ CompositeA market-capitalization-weighted index of all stocks listed on the NASDAQ exchange, with a heavy concentration in technology and growth-oriented companies.
NYSE CompositeA market-capitalization-weighted index of all common stocks listed on the New York Stock Exchange, providing a broad view of large, established U.S. companies.
Russell Midcap IndexA market-capitalization-weighted index measuring the performance of the 800 smallest companies in the Russell 1000 Index, representing the mid-cap segment of the U.S. equity market.
Russell Top 200 IndexA market-capitalization-weighted index of the 200 largest U.S. companies by market cap, representing the mega-cap segment of the domestic equity market.
S&P 500 IndexA market-capitalization-weighted index of 500 of the largest publicly traded U.S. companies, widely regarded as the primary benchmark for the U.S. stock market.
Value Line Geometric IndexAn equally-weighted geometric average of approximately 1,700 U.S. stocks across all market capitalizations, making it more representative of how a typical stock performs than market-cap-weighted indices.
Global Equity Indices
Dow Jones Developed Markets Index (DJ DM Index)A market-capitalization-weighted index tracking equity markets in developed countries outside the United States, used as a benchmark for international developed market performance.
Dow Jones Emerging Markets Index (DJ EM Index)A market-capitalization-weighted index tracking stock markets in developing economies across Asia, Latin America, Eastern Europe, Africa, and the Middle East.
MSCI All Country World Index (ACWI)A market-capitalization-weighted index covering equities from 23 developed and 24 emerging market countries, representing approximately 85% of the global investable equity market.
MSCI Equal-Weighted ACWIA version of the MSCI ACWI in which each country or constituent is given equal weight, reducing the dominance of large markets and providing a more balanced view of global equity performance.
MSCI World IndexA market-capitalization-weighted index of stocks from 23 developed market countries, excluding emerging markets, used as the primary benchmark for global developed market equities.
Fixed Income Benchmarks
Barclays Global Aggregate Bond IndexA broad benchmark measuring the global investment-grade fixed income market, including government, corporate, and securitized bonds from developed and emerging markets.
High Yield Corporate BondsDebt securities issued by companies with below-investment-grade credit ratings (also called 'junk bonds'), which offer higher yields to compensate investors for greater default risk.
Moody's Aaa Corporate Bond YieldThe average yield on the highest-rated investment-grade corporate bonds, representing the borrowing cost for the most financially sound companies and used as a benchmark for top-tier corporate credit.
Moody's Baa Corporate Bond YieldThe average yield on bonds rated Baa by Moody's Investors Service, representing the lowest rung of investment-grade corporate debt, commonly used as a proxy for broad corporate credit conditions.
U.S. Treasury SecuritiesDebt obligations issued by the U.S. federal government across a range of maturities — from 3-month T-bills to 30-year Treasury bonds — considered the benchmark for risk-free interest rates in the United States.
Volatility Measures
CBOE Volatility Index (VIX)A real-time index published by the Chicago Board Options Exchange measuring the market's 30-day implied volatility expectation for the S&P 500, derived from options prices and widely known as the market's 'fear gauge.'
Currency
U.S. Dollar Index (DXY)A measure of the value of the U.S. dollar relative to a basket of six major foreign currencies — the euro, Japanese yen, British pound, Canadian dollar, Swedish krona, and Swiss franc.
Commodities
Reuters Continuous Commodity Index (CCI)An equally-weighted index of 17 commodity futures contracts spanning agricultural products, energy, and metals, used as a broad measure of overall commodity price trends.
Economic Indicators
Challenger Job Cut ReportA monthly report by Challenger, Gray & Christmas tracking the number of layoff announcements made by U.S. employers, used as a leading indicator of labor market health and business confidence.
Consumer Price Index (CPI)A monthly measure of the average change in prices paid by urban consumers for a representative basket of goods and services, published by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics and the primary gauge of U.S. inflation.
Federal Reserve Free ReservesThe amount of reserves that commercial banks hold above the minimum required by regulators, serving as an indicator of the banking system's capacity and willingness to extend credit.
Industrial Production IndexA monthly measure published by the Federal Reserve tracking real output from U.S. manufacturing, mining, and electric and gas utilities sectors.
ISM Manufacturing Purchasing Managers' Index (PMI)A monthly survey by the Institute for Supply Management of U.S. manufacturing purchasing managers, where readings above 50 indicate sector expansion and below 50 indicate contraction.
ISM Non-Manufacturing Index (NMI) / Services IndexA monthly survey by the Institute for Supply Management of U.S. service sector businesses, where readings above 50 indicate expansion and below 50 indicate contraction.
ISM Price IndexA component of the ISM Manufacturing survey tracking whether companies are paying more or less for inputs and raw materials, used as a leading indicator of producer-level inflation.
M1 Money SupplyThe narrowest measure of the U.S. money supply, comprising physical currency in circulation, demand deposits, and other highly liquid instruments held by the public.
M2 Money SupplyA broader measure of the U.S. money supply that includes M1 plus savings deposits, small time deposits, and retail money market funds, widely used as a gauge of overall monetary conditions.
Nonfarm Unit Labor CostsA quarterly measure of the labor cost required to produce one unit of output in the U.S. nonfarm business sector, published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics and used to assess wage-driven inflationary pressure on corporate profit margins.
Producer Price Index (PPI) for CommoditiesA measure of the average change in prices received by domestic producers for commodity goods at the wholesale level, published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics and used as an early-stage inflation indicator.
Sentiment & Survey Indicators
AAII Investor Sentiment SurveyA weekly survey by the American Association of Individual Investors asking members whether they are bullish, neutral, or bearish on the stock market over the next six months, commonly used as a contrarian indicator.
Citigroup U.S. Economic Surprise IndexA proprietary index published by Citigroup measuring the degree to which recent U.S. economic data releases beat or miss consensus analyst forecasts, with positive readings indicating data is coming in better than expected.
Conference Board Consumer Confidence SurveyA monthly survey by The Conference Board measuring U.S. consumer attitudes about current business conditions, employment, and their expectations for the economy over the next six months.
Hulbert Financial Digest / Newsletter Sentiment IndexA long-running service tracking the average recommended stock market exposure across a broad group of financial newsletter writers, used as a contrarian sentiment indicator.
Investor's Intelligence Advisors Sentiment SurveyA weekly survey measuring the bullish-versus-bearish split among U.S. investment newsletter writers, used since the 1960s as a contrarian indicator of excessive optimism or pessimism.
Sentix Investor Confidence SurveyA weekly survey of approximately 5,000 institutional and individual investors across major global markets measuring their current economic sentiment and six-month expectations.
Market Breadth Indicators
McClellan Summation IndexA long-term breadth indicator that is a cumulative running total of the McClellan Oscillator, measuring the sustained trend of advancing versus declining stocks on the NYSE.
NDR Proprietary Indices & Models
NDR Big Mo Multi-Cap Tape CompositeNed Davis Research's primary measure of internal U.S. market strength, combining trend and momentum signals across small-, mid-, and large-cap sub-industry group indices into a single 0–100% reading.
NDR ETF Speculation IndexA proprietary Ned Davis Research contrarian sentiment index aggregating ETF flow, positioning, and sentiment data into a standardized score to identify extremes of investor optimism or pessimism.
NDR Equal-Dollar Weighted Equity SeriesA proprietary Ned Davis Research index weighting U.S. stocks equally in dollar terms across multiple market capitalizations, used to measure broad equity market momentum and overbought/oversold conditions.
NDR Multi-Cap Institutional Equity SeriesA proprietary Ned Davis Research index family tracking institutional equity performance across small-, mid-, and large-cap segments, used in breadth, volume, and momentum analysis.
NDR Sub-Industry Group IndicesA proprietary set of Ned Davis Research indices tracking price performance at the sub-industry level across the U.S. equity market, used as the building blocks for the Big Mo Tape Composite.
NDR Top WatchA proprietary Ned Davis Research model comprising nine components that monitors market conditions historically associated with global equity declines of 10% or more.
